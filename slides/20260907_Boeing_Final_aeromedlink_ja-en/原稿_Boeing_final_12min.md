## 1. Cover — 0:30

Good morning. We are Team AeroMedLink from Chiba University.

For the next twelve minutes, we would like to take you through how we got to our idea.

Not the idea first. The way we got there.

▸ 最初の3秒は黙って会場を見る。急がない。
▸ "Not the idea first." の前で1拍おく。今日の構成の予告になる。

---

## 2. Where we started — 1:00

We expected to hear that aircraft need better medical devices.

That is not what we heard.

▸ ここでスライドの引用を指す。読み上げる前に2秒待つ。

“The doctor asked me for her blood pressure.
All I could give them was how she looked.”

Think about that sentence for a moment.

A doctor is on the line. The doctor asks the one question every doctor asks first:
what are the numbers? And the answer is a description. Pale. Sweating. Looks unwell.

Nobody was careless. The crew did everything right.
They simply had no number to give — so they gave words instead.
And that is where the whole problem starts.

▸ "what are the numbers?" を強く。ここが本発表の芯。
▸ 「乗務員が悪いのではない」を必ず言う。現場を責める発表にしない。

---

## 3. Today — 1:05

This is what happens next, and this is the picture we want you to remember.

▸ 図を出したまま3秒黙る。会場に図を読ませる。

On the left, inside the aircraft. On the right, on the ground.

The numbers the doctor asked for are at the top — and they are question marks.
Nobody can fill them in, so nothing reliable ever crosses to the right-hand side.

What crosses instead is words. The crew tell the purser.
The purser tells the captain. The captain reaches the doctor.
And when the aircraft lands, the ambulance team asks all of it again.

Look at the people. They are all connected — radios, interphones, satellite calls.
The people are connected. The information is not.

▸ 最後の2文はスライドの見出しと同じ。ゆっくり、区切って。
▸ "told four times, and thinner every time" は takeaway を指しながら。

---

## 4. Already on board — 0:45

Then we looked at what the aircraft already carries. This surprised us.

Every large airliner carries an emergency medical kit — a blood-pressure cuff,
a stethoscope, and eleven different medicines. It carries a defibrillator.
And it carries a satellite link that is already selling internet to passengers.

So the cuff is on board. The doctor is on the line. And still, no number gets across.

Everything is already on board. Nothing is connected.

▸ 「道具はある。つながっていないだけ」——ここで問題の性質が変わる。
▸ 最後の一文はスライドと同じ文。ゆっくり。

---

## 5. So we asked one question — 0:20

So our question became very simple.

What if the information was connected too?

▸ ここで完全に止まる。3秒。次のスライドまで何も言わない。

---

## 6. Our answer — 1:10

This is AeroMedLink, and this is the same picture as before — with one thing added.

▸ 図を出す。左右が「機内」「地上」で前のスライドと同じ配置だと気づかせる。

In the middle, there is now one shared record.

The cabin crew measure once, with certified sensors, and the numbers go in.
Not a description — ninety-six, eighty-two, one twenty-eight over seventy-six.

From that moment, everyone is looking at the same page.
The purser sees it instead of being told. The captain sees it instead of relaying it.
The ground physician reads it before the call even starts.
And the ambulance team has it before the aircraft lands.

The whole cabin and the doctor outside meet on one record. Everything meets here.

And we want to be very clear about one thing.
The system does not decide anything. Told once, connected to everyone —
and still decided by the same people.

▸ 数字は一度だけ、ゆっくり読む。ここで「?」が埋まったことを聴衆に体感させる。
▸ 最後の一文は審査員が一番気にする点。目を上げて言う。

---

## 7. In the cabin — 0:55

In the cabin, it is four steps.

Step one, before the flight: a passenger can register an optional profile —
allergies, conditions, medication. Optional, and only if they want to.

Step two: the crew measure. The screen tells them what to do, one step at a time.
They do not have to remember a protocol during an emergency.

Step three: the doctor opens the same page. There is no briefing call,
because there is nothing left to brief.

Step four: the captain decides — as they always have — and that same record
goes ahead to the ambulance team, before the aircraft lands.

We are not adding a medical role to the cabin crew.
We are letting them tell it once, and go back to the patient.

▸ 手順はテンポを上げてよい。ここで止まらない。

---

## 8. Two calls — 1:05

Let us show you two calls.

Case A. Shortness of breath during cruise. Oxygen ninety-six, ninety-seven percent.
The pulse settles. The symptoms improve. The doctor sees the trend — not a description —
and says: stable, continue and monitor. The flight reaches its destination.

Case B. Chest pain that does not stop. Blood pressure falling.
The doctor says: urgent ground evaluation. The aircraft diverts —
and the patient's record arrives before the patient does.

Same system. Two opposite answers.
Fewer diversions we did not need. Faster ones when we do.

▸ AとBはトーンを変える。Aは落ち着いて、Bは少し速く。
▸ 「安く済ませる話ではない」と示す決め所。

---

## 9. How often — 0:45

How often does this happen? More often than we expected.

One flight in six hundred has a medical emergency on board.
One in twenty-two of those ends with the aircraft landing somewhere
it was never going to land.

Four billion people fly every year. That is two hundred emergencies a day, worldwide —
about two a day at Haneda alone.

Rare for one passenger. Routine for the industry.

▸ 数字はゆっくり、はっきり。ここだけは速く言わない。

---

## 10. What it costs — 0:55

And a diversion is expensive.

Fuel. Landing fees. Crew hours. Hotels. Compensation.
Between fifteen thousand and nine hundred thousand US dollars — for one landing.

At nine diversions a day, that is up to seven point eight million dollars. Every day.
As an industry, that is the price of a brand new 787 every forty-two days.

Now, some of those landings save lives, and we would make them again tomorrow.
But some of them were avoidable — and connected information is how we tell which.

▸ "Every day." は独立した文として、間を置いてから。
▸ ダイバートを悪者にしていないことを必ず伝える。

---

## 11. Feasibility — 0:50

Is this realistic? We think so, for three reasons.

The parts already exist. Certified sensors, off the shelf.
We assemble proven components — we do not invent new medical devices.

The link is already flying. Our data is tiny compared to passenger video.

And the market already pays. Airlines contract ground medical support today,
so there is a customer, and a price to compare with.

Using proven parts is what keeps both the risk and the certification manageable.

---

## 12. Hard questions — 1:00

We also want to be honest about what is hard. Three problems we could not ignore.

First, law. A doctor cannot legally diagnose across every border.
So in our design, the doctor advises. The captain and the local medical team decide.
Defining that role clearly removes most of the legal barrier.

Second, the crew. Cabin crew are not medical staff.
So the device has to be simple: guided steps on screen, simple sensors, standard training.
If it is not usable in a real emergency, it is not a solution.

Third, approval. Medical devices and aircraft equipment both need certification.
So we use devices that are already certified, start in one country — Japan —
and expand together with the regulators.

▸ first / second / third を明確に。質疑はほぼここから来る。

---

## 13. Value — 0:40

So why does this matter? Because it takes work off everyone in the chain.

The crew stop guessing — they read a number instead of finding the words.
The doctor stops interviewing — the numbers are there before the call starts.
The captain stops deciding blind — same authority, better evidence.

Less work for everyone. And the passenger is helped sooner.

▸ 三者を指で数える。ここが「なぜ重要か」の答え。
▸ 最後の一文をこの発表で一番はっきり言う。

---

## 14. Why Boeing — 0:25

And why Boeing? Because an airline can buy a medical service from anyone —
but only Boeing can make the aircraft ready for it.

The link is already there. This is what it is for.
Boeing does not have to build a medical company. It has to open the door.

▸ 短く、言い切る。長く説明しない。

---

## 15. Closing — 0:15

From isolated emergencies — to connected care.

Thank you.

▸ "From isolated emergencies" のあとに2秒。最後の "Thank you." は笑顔で。

## 質疑応答の想定（英語で1文ずつ用意しておく）

| 想定質問 | 一言で返す軸 |
|---|---|
| Who is legally responsible if the advice is wrong? | The doctor advises; the captain and the local medical team hold the authority. That is unchanged from today. |
